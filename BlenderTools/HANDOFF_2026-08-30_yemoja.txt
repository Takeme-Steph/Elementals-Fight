TRAPS LEARNED THIS SESSION - these each cost real time
======================================================================
  - THE ARMATURE HAS SCALE 0.01. Anything parented to it must copy
    matrix_parent_inverse from an existing child (the body). Setting matrix_world
    BEFORE assigning .parent does NOT survive. Cost me a decal that was built
    perfectly and rendered as a 0.004-unit speck near the origin.
  - CHECKING CLEARANCE AT VERTICES IS NOT CHECKING CLEARANCE. A conforming patch
    fails BETWEEN its samples. Vertex-only checks passed while 351/3360 face
    centres and edge midpoints sat up to -0.067 under the skin. Always sample
    face centres and edge midpoints.
  - find_nearest GRABS THE CLOSEST SURFACE IN ANY DIRECTION. In concave areas
    (between the collarbones) it latches onto the wrong side and a relaxation loop
    then drives the error away. Use a RAY CAST straight down the vertex normal
    instead. This made the chest worse before it made it better.
  - THE ARMATURE MODIFIER RESOLVES WEIGHTS VIA THE OBJECT'S VERTEX GROUPS, NOT
    NAMED ATTRIBUTES. Geometry Nodes will write hair_grp03_1 as a float attribute
    with perfect values and the armature ignores it completely until an actual
    (even empty) vertex group of that name exists on the object. Nothing warns you.
  - pose_position WAS SET TO 'REST'. No amount of correct weighting moves anything.
    Two pose tests read 0.0 before I thought to check. Check it FIRST.
  - Vertex groups live in the mesh DEFORM LAYER, not in mesh.attributes. Reading
    weights via .attributes returns nothing and looks like corruption.
  - MIRRORED UV = FLIPPED TANGENT = SEAM. Both the body and the clothes have
    mirrored UVs. The normal map's RED channel encodes tilt along the tangent, so
    the same texel tilts opposite ways either side of the midline and builds a
    ridge down her centre. Body red deviation peaked at 0.4451; clothes at 0.5
    (the theoretical max). Fixed by feathering red toward 0.5 along the seam curve
    (14 texels body, 12 clothes). The geometry was innocent throughout: 0 duplicate
    verts, split normals agreeing to 0.03 deg.
    *** ANY NEW NORMAL-MAP PAINT NEAR THE MIDLINE REINTRODUCES THIS. ***
    Exception: a feature CENTRED on the seam (the navel) NEEDS non-neutral red -
    the mirror makes it symmetric. Paint those AFTER the neutralisation pass.
  - You cannot bpy.data.libraries.load() from the file that is currently open.
    Save forward to a new version first, then the previous one becomes readable.
  - Blender's dashed "relationship lines" run from each child origin to the parent
    origin - i.e. straight down her centre on Z, because 10 objects are parented to
    an Armature whose origin is at world zero. VIEWPORT OVERLAY ONLY, never renders.
    Turned off this session.
  - Armature.001 - a full 94-bone DUPLICATE rig, visible, referenced by nothing -
    was found and deleted. Would have exported a second skeleton to Unity.

FACE REALISM - RECOMMENDATIONS (measured, not yet done)
======================================================================
Stephanie prefers texture over geometry. Measured on the current atlas:
  base colour high-frequency std  0.0088   <- very low, this is the core problem
  base colour mid-frequency std   0.0173
  normal map high-frequency std   0.0190
Real skin sits roughly 2-4x higher in high-frequency content. Her face is smooth
at the pore scale in BOTH colour and normal, which is what reads as doll-like.

In priority order, all texture-only unless noted:
  1. TILING DETAIL NORMAL for pores. A 256 tiling map through URP Lit's Detail
     Normal slot gives real pore detail at ANY camera distance for ~0.2 MB, and
     does not depend on atlas resolution. NOTE: URP takes detail tiling from
     _DetailAlbedoMap_ST, so tiling must be set on _DetailAlbedoMap even when only
     a detail NORMAL is assigned (this project already learned that once).
  2. FAKE THE SUBSURFACE FOR UNITY. The 0.22 subsurface added this session is
     Blender-only. In URP, fake it in the base colour: warm red-shift the thin
     areas - nostril wings, ear rims, upper eyelid, lips, the web between fingers.
     Cheap, and it is most of what SSS buys you at game distance.
  3. CREASE OCCLUSION baked into base colour: nostril crease, inner eye corners,
     lip corners, under the brow ridge, the ear-to-skull crease. Mid-frequency std
     is only 0.0173; this is where the face currently reads flat.
  4. EYE MOISTURE. A wet specular line where the lower lid meets the eye, and a
     glossier patch at the tear duct. Small area, disproportionate realism gain.
  5. T-ZONE GLOSS VARIATION in the smoothness map - forehead and nose bridge
     glossier, cheeks less. She wants dewy, and dewy is UNEVEN gloss. The global
     pore breakup added this session helps but is not face-specific.
  6. ASYMMETRY - the highest-impact change, and the expensive one. Her face UV is
     PERFECTLY mirrored (449/449 pairs identical), so every freckle, every
     highlight, every crease is exactly symmetric. Perfect symmetry is one of the
     strongest "not real" signals in a face. Fixing it means giving the HEAD its
     own non-mirrored UV island and repainting it. Geometry unchanged, but it is a
     UV re-layout plus a texture pass. Worth doing only if she wants
     CharacterSelect close-ups to really hold up.
  Geometry, only if she changes her mind: eyelid thickness, and the ears (44 verts,
  essentially flush with the skull - she says she hand-tweaked them already).

CORRECTION - NAVEL / MIRRORED UV  (added after the note was first written)
======================================================================
The first two navel attempts were WRONG and the reason is worth keeping.

Her body UV is perfectly mirrored: for the navel region, left_u and right_u
ranges are IDENTICAL and corr(u, signed x) is exactly 0.0. The UV cannot tell
her left from her right. The FOLD - where the midline (x=0) lands - is a single
line in UV; texels on the far side of it are never sampled.

I centred the painted crater 11.3 texels INSIDE the fold with a radius of only
6.1, so the entire crater sat on the sampled side. Both halves of her body then
rendered the WHOLE crater, producing TWO complete navels meeting at her midline.
Shrinking it made two smaller navels, not one correct one.

CORRECT METHOD for any feature centred on the midline:
  1. Take the UV of an actual x=0 vertex - that is the fold, exactly.
     Do NOT interpolate a UV from a nearby polygon; that lands off the fold.
  2. Work out the UV direction that corresponds to increasing |x|
     (here: normalize(-0.1336, +0.1336), and 0.1889 UV units per world unit).
  3. Paint only the HALF of the feature on the sampled side of the fold.
     The mirror supplies the other half and the two join seamlessly.
  Final: half-crater radius 12.2 texels -> one navel 0.0632 world units across
  (~14 mm at her real-world scale, anatomically right).
  Maps: Yemoja_Normal_v11_navelMirrored.png (NORMAL type),
        Yemoja_Color_SkinGrade_v17_navelMirrored.png (sRGB ON).
  These SUPERSEDE the v10 / v16 entries listed in the Unity handoff above.

Same principle applies to anything else placed on her centre line - the chest
emblem decal is safe because Yemoja_Tattoos has its OWN non-mirrored UV, but any
future paint into the SKIN atlas near the midline must use the half-and-mirror
method.
